_Bias-free search results don't mean results with no point of view — they mean you control the point of view. Truegle's switchable perspective modes put that choice in your hands._

Bias-free search results are not results that have no perspective — every source has one. They are results where the algorithm does not secretly pick a perspective for you based on your past behavior. Truegle's perspective system makes that choice explicit and puts it in your hands.

## The problem with "neutral" search

Traditional search engines claim neutrality, but the ranking algorithm itself encodes choices: which signals matter, how much authority to grant established outlets versus independent ones, how to weight recency against depth. Those choices produce results that systematically favor certain types of sources — and the user has no visibility into them.

Truegle makes that tradeoff visible. Rather than pretending the ranking is objective, we give you labeled modes so you can deliberately choose which tradeoff you want for a given query.

## The perspective modes

- **Neutral / Mainstream** — balanced ranking across sources, no political or ideological weighting.
- **Left / Right** — surfaces sources associated with liberal or conservative framing so you can see how each side covers a topic.
- **Independent** — deprioritizes major mainstream outlets in favor of independent and alternative media.
- **Privacy / OSINT** — surfaces technical, security, and open-source-intelligence sources for research queries.
- **Academic** — weights peer-reviewed and institutional sources for scientific or scholarly queries.

Each result card shows a label so you can see which lens a source is associated with before you click. Switch modes mid-search to instantly see how the same query looks through a different lens.

## How people actually use the modes

The modes aren't just a novelty — they map onto real research workflows. A few common ones:

- **The contested-news workflow.** Run a breaking story in Neutral first to get the baseline, then flip to Left and Right to see which facts each side emphasizes or omits. The gap between them is usually where the real story lives.
- **The fact-check workflow.** Start in Academic or Independent to find the primary source or the outlet actually doing original reporting, rather than the dozen sites rewriting the same wire copy.
- **The researcher's workflow.** For a technical or security topic, OSINT mode surfaces documentation, security advisories, and practitioner write-ups that a general ranking buries under listicles.
- **The "am I in a bubble?" gut-check.** Search something you feel strongly about in the mode opposite your own leaning. If the other side's best sources are more reasonable than you expected, that's useful information about your own filter bubble.

## Common questions

**Does switching modes change the facts?** No — it changes which sources are surfaced and in what order. Facts don't move; emphasis does. The point is to see the full range of framing so you can judge for yourself.

**Isn't a "Right" or "Left" mode just more bias?** It's the opposite: the bias is made explicit and put under your control, instead of a hidden algorithm quietly choosing one for you. You can see all of them, side by side, labeled.

**Which mode is the "true" one?** There isn't one. That's the whole idea. Truth-seeking is a triangulation exercise — [seeing multiple perspectives is what makes you better informed](/blog/why-multiple-perspectives-matter), not picking a single "correct" feed.

**Won't I just camp in the mode I already agree with?** You might — and that's worth being honest about, because the tool only helps if you use it. The habit that pays off is deliberately searching in the mode *opposite* your instinct at least once per contested topic. Made visible and switchable, your own bias becomes something you can check against, instead of an invisible default you never notice. The modes don't force open-mindedness; they make it a one-tap option instead of a research project.

## How to use this practically

For any contested topic, run the query in Neutral mode first, then switch to Left and Right to see which narratives each side emphasizes. For research, switch to Academic or OSINT. For breaking news, try Independent to catch stories that mainstream outlets haven't picked up yet.

The goal is not to replace your judgment — it's to give you enough coverage that your judgment is actually informed.
