# Product Hunt

> **Draft status:** raw material for the owner. Most Product Hunt guidance online is from launch-tool vendors, not Product Hunt itself, and it disagrees on details. Check producthunt.com's own submission page before you launch.

## Honest fit

Product Hunt's audience is mostly builders and early adopters. Truegle is a consumer search engine, so expect modest results. Hacker News and the privacy communities are likely better first bets. If you do launch, do it once; Product Hunt may ask whether a re-submission within about six months is a major update.

## No CLI

Submission is a web form (Submit a Product). There is no practical CLI route for posting.

## Before launch day

- Use a maker account with real history; guides advise against launching from a fresh account. Do not ask people to upvote.
- The repo is MIT licensed now (merged), so you can list it as open source.
- Have 3-5 real screenshots (home, results with source labels, lens switcher) and, if you like, a short screen recording. No mock-ups.
- Pick Tuesday-Thursday; many guides suggest scheduling for 12:01 AM Pacific, but this is secondhand advice.

## Description (234 chars; confirm the field's current limit)

> Truegle is a private metasearch engine. It queries several providers, labels each result by source, and lets you switch between mainstream, independent and zero-AI lenses. No ads, no cookies. Young one-developer project, MIT licensed.

## First maker comment (post it yourself; edit freely)

> Hi, I'm the developer. I built Truegle because I wanted a private search engine that shows where each result comes from instead of one blended list. It queries several providers (Brave among them) through a self-hosted SearXNG layer and labels the sources; the lenses let you compare a mainstream and an independent view of the same query, and there's a zero-AI mode.
>
> What it isn't: it has no index of its own, so it depends on the providers it combines, and they see the query text (not your IP or an account). Ordinary searches are kept as an anonymous line for up to 7 days to show what's trending. Source: https://github.com/o87enterprises-ai/TruegleSearch (MIT).
>
> The thing I most want feedback on: is the source labeling useful, or just noise?
