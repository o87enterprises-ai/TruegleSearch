# Outreach to "best private search engines" articles

> **Draft status:** guidance for the owner. Contact details must come from each site's own editorial or contact page; none are filled in here.

## What is realistic

Articles that rank for "best private search engines" are mostly written by VPN vendors, for example [NordVPN](https://nordvpn.com/blog/private-search-engines/), [Nym](https://nym.com/blog/most-private-search-engines), [Private Internet Access](https://www.privateinternetaccess.com/blog/private-search-engines/), [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/private-search-engine/) and similar blogs. They tend to list the same names (DuckDuckGo, Brave Search, Mojeek, Startpage) and often favor engines with their own index. A one-year-old metasearch project is a long shot for a first-page slot. Expect most emails to go unanswered.

Better odds:

- **Smaller blogs, newsletters and YouTube channels** that cover open-source privacy tools, metasearch engines, or "alternatives to Google". Search for recent posts about MetaGer, SearXNG, Mojeek or Brave Search and see who writes them.
- **Articles that are plainly out of date** or missing a metasearch category, where a short factual note is useful to the author.
- **Comparison posts** where your own comparison page ([Truegle vs DuckDuckGo vs Startpage](https://truegle.info/blog/truegle-vs-duckduckgo-vs-startpage/)) gives them something citable.

## Rules to keep

- **Never pay for placement** or offer money or free accounts in exchange for a mention. Paid links must be disclosed and it undermines the trust you want.
- **One tailored email per author.** No mass mailing, no templates sent unchanged, no follow-up more than once.
- **State plainly that you built it**, and give the limits (young, one developer, no own index, providers see query text).
- **Only claims they can check**: link to the privacy policy, the source repo, and the comparison page.
- **Use a contact address you choose to share.** Don't paste a personal address into anything public; the placeholder below is intentional.

## Email template (edit for each person; keep it short)

Subject: Fact check / suggestion for your "[article title]" article

> Hi [name],
>
> I read your article "[title]" and found [specific thing that was useful]. I'm the developer of Truegle (https://truegle.info), a small private metasearch engine, so I'm writing with that bias up front.
>
> One thing it does differently from the engines in your list: it queries several providers (Brave among them) and labels each result by source, with lenses for comparing mainstream and independent views. It has no ads or cookies, and the code is MIT licensed: https://github.com/o87enterprises-ai/TruegleSearch . It's young and has no index of its own, and providers see the query text; the privacy policy spells out what's stored: https://truegle.info/privacy/
>
> If it's useful, I wrote an honest side-by-side with DuckDuckGo and Startpage here: https://truegle.info/blog/truegle-vs-duckduckgo-vs-startpage/
>
> No expectation of a mention. If you'd like to try it or have questions, I'm happy to help.
>
> [your name] ([your contact email])

## Tracking

Keep a simple list (site, author, date emailed, reply) so you never email the same person twice. A spreadsheet is enough.
